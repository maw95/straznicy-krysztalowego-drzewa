# Gleamroot: Guardians of the Crystal Tree

*An adventure game for 1–6 players, ages 5 and up. A game lasts from a quarter of an hour to an hour.*

---

## The Tale of the Tree

Long ago, before the first rivers found their way to the sea, the **Crystal Tree** grew on the top of the world. Its roots reached deeper than the oldest caves, and its leaves of pure crystal caught the light of the stars and scattered it across the whole land. Thanks to its power the earth stayed fertile, the waters clear and the forests full of life.

The Tree watched over the balance between all living creatures. Unicorns roamed the blooming meadows, griffins soared above the mountain peaks, and wolves and bats lived in the vast forests and in caves hidden among the rocks. Every creature had its place, and none had to fight for the right to exist. So the world endured, a world where the light belonged to everyone.

Until one night the **Black Dragon** appeared over the land, the eldest of the children of darkness. Jealous of the power of the Crystal Tree, he longed to take all its light for himself. He struck the trunk with such force that the crown trembled, and the three **Crystals of Care**, the source of its ancient power, fell from between the crystal branches and scattered across the world.

Ever since, that night has been known as the **Night of the Lost Glow**.

Robbed of its three crystals, the Tree began to weaken. Its leaves slowly lost their shine, the rivers grew murky, and the once green lands began to wither. The old balance was shaken, and the shadow of the Black Dragon reached further and further.

Meanwhile each of the crystals ended up in a different place. The first came to rest in a cave where a greedy cyclops hoarded his glittering treasures. The second caught the eye of a griffin, who carried it off to a nest full of valuables. The third sank among drowned ruins guarded by a many-headed hydra.

Until the Crystals of Care return to the Crystal Tree, its power will not be fully reborn, and the land will sink ever deeper into darkness. **The fate of the whole world now depends on whether the lost crystals can be recovered and the balance restored before it is too late.**

So two powers set out on the road.

**The Dawn Covenant** — the Golden Dragon, the Unicorn King and the Ancient Bear with their friends — wants to find the crystals and give them back to the Tree before it goes out forever.

**The Dark Horde** — the Black Dragon, the Werewolf Lord and the Ogre Iron Fist with their retinue — wants to stop them. And if it manages to stand beside the Tree itself and perform a ritual there, the Tree's last light will belong to the darkness forever.

The crystals are watched by the **Guardians** — creatures neither good nor evil, who simply do not like giving back what has fallen into their paws.

Choose a side. The Tree is waiting.

## Rules in pictures

*The whole game in eight pictures — for the first game, for the youngest players and for everyone who does not like reading rulebooks. The details are in the later chapters.*

1. **The Covenant carries the crystals to the Tree.** It takes 2 crystals when the Covenant has one hero, and 3 when it has two.
2. **The Horde wins when time runs out.** The crystal marker moves along the round track; after the last round of the map (8 / 12 / 18 / 24) the Horde wins. It also wins when it defeats all the Covenant's heroes or performs the ritual at the Tree.
3. **On its turn a hero has 3 movement points.** Easy terrain costs 1 point, hard terrain 2, and an obstacle cannot be entered.
4. **Stepped onto a hex? Flip the face-down tiles around it.** That is how the land is revealed: treasures, guardian lairs and crystals.
5. **Step onto an enemy's hex and a fight begins.** A Covenant hero can also challenge a crystal guardian.
6. **The faster one strikes first.** With equal Speed, the one who is defending goes first.
7. **A blow deals as much damage as the Attack value.** Put drops on the card. When the damage equals the Health, the unit falls.
8. **A defeated guardian gives up its crystal and a treasure.** The hero picks up the crystal at once, and the Covenant draws a treasure.

## 1. What is in the box

The quantities are enough for the most demanding game: a very long map and a 3-on-3 battle.

- **Map tiles — 121** (5 cm hexagons):
  - **50 Covenant terrain tiles** with a green back: 7 of each of 7 kinds (Glade, Forest, River, Bridge, Hills, Mountains, Chasm) and 1 extra Glade;
  - **49 Horde terrain tiles** with a burgundy back: 7 of each of 7 kinds (Scorched Earth, Barren Plain, Dark Swamp, Dark Cave, Grim Ruins, Dead Thicket, Poison Thorns);
  - **16 treasure tiles**: 8 with a green back and 8 with a burgundy back;
  - **3 guardian lairs**: the Griffin's (green back), the Cyclops' and the Hydra's (burgundy back);
  - the **Crystal Tree** and **two bases**: the Covenant's and the Horde's.
- **Double-sided arena board** (A3, folds in half) — on one side the green arena of the Covenant, on the other the fiery arena of the Horde. Each side has 7 obstacles, and the outermost columns are the starting hexes.
- **18 unit cards** (8 × 11.2 cm): 6 Covenant (3 heroes, 3 supports), 6 Horde (3 chiefs, 3 henchmen), 3 guardians (navy blue back) and 3 guardian cards for the duel (sky blue back, chapter 9).
- **15 figures** — one for each unit (the same 15 characters as on the cards). Each stands on a 3 cm base that fits in an arena hex and on a map tile.
- **20 treasure cards** (6.3 × 8.8 cm): 6 Elixirs of Rebirth and 1 each of the other 14 treasures.
- **3 crystals** (1.6 cm tall).
- **Round track** — a hexagonal board (15 cm) with the Tree in the middle and 24 slots — and the **crystal round marker** (3 cm tall, easy to lift out of a slot).
- **Tokens** (enough for one battle; the round ones are 2.3 cm):
  - **damage drops**, double-sided: 6 × "1" / "2" and 10 × "3" / "5";
  - **used ability**: 6;
  - **Attack Boost (+1 ATK)**: 6; **Vitality (+4 HP)**: 1; **Protection**: 1;
  - **+3 Attack** (Golden Dragon after Sunlight): 1;
  - **Immobilisation**, **Slow (−2 SPD)**, **Curse (−1 ATK)**, **Quicken (+3 SPD)**: 1 each.
- **Die** −1 / 0 / +1 — only for the "Dice roll" hardship (chapter 13).

Every unit has three numbers:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| ❤ **Health (HP)** | how much damage it can take | when the damage equals the Health, the unit falls |
| ⚔ **Attack (ATK)** | how much damage it deals with one blow | |
| ⚡ **Speed (SPD)** | how many hexes it moves in battle | faster units act first |

and one **type**: it fights **in melee** (swords), **shoots** (bow) or **flies** (wing).

## 2. Who is who — units

Each faction has **three heroes** and **three supports**. Each player leads one hero. The supports belong to the whole faction — before every battle a hero takes two of them along.

### Dawn Covenant

> *A green banner with a golden tree. Those who remember what the land looked like when the Tree shone at full glow.*

**🐉 Golden Dragon** — hero, flying · HP 9 · ATK 5 · SPD 4
The eldest of the bright dragons. He sleeps on mountain peaks and wakes only when the land truly needs him. They say his scales remember the colour of the first dawn.
**Sunlight** — once per battle: every living Covenant unit gets +1 ATK on its next attack, and the Dragon himself +3 ATK. *(+3 Attack token)*

**🦄 Unicorn King** — hero, melee · HP 8 · ATK 5 · SPD 8
Lord of the silver herds of the meadows beneath the Tree. He never attacks first, but once he charges, the earth trembles to the horizon.
**Charge of Light** — always, not once per battle: +2 ATK to an attack made after moving at least 4 hexes.

**🐻 Ancient Bear** — hero, melee · HP 11 · ATK 4 · SPD 4
Guardian of the primeval forest, with birds living in his fur and ferns growing in his tracks. Slow and patient like the trees themselves — and just as hard to knock over.
**Grove Guardian** — once per battle, when the Bear or an adjacent ally is about to take a deadly blow: the Bear takes it on himself and ignores 3 damage.

**🏹 Elf Druid** — support, shooter · HP 4 · ATK 1 · SPD 4
Talks to roots and listens to what the moss whispers. His arrows rarely wound badly, but they can bind the legs of any enemy.
**Tangled Roots** — once per battle: the enemy hit has −2 SPD in its next activation. *(Slow token)*

**🌸 Nymph** — support, melee · HP 5 · ATK 2 · SPD 4
Keeper of the springs. Wherever she passes, flowers bloom and wounds heal faster.
**Gift of Life** — once per battle: an ally regains 3 HP (no more than the card allows).

**🪽 Pegasus** — support, flying · HP 4 · ATK 2 · SPD 4
A winged horse from the clouds above the Tree. It strikes from above and vanishes before the enemy can turn around.
**Air Rush** — once per battle: after attacking, it may fly away up to 3 hexes.

### Dark Horde

> *A burgundy banner with a horned helmet. Those who believe that the light of the Tree belongs to the strongest.*

The Horde calls its heroes **chiefs** and its supports **henchmen**. In the rules they are simply heroes and supports — everything that applies to heroes and supports applies to them in the same way.

**🐲 Black Dragon** — chief, flying · HP 7 · ATK 5 · SPD 5
It was he who struck the Tree on the Night of the Lost Glow. Since then his breath smells of ash and his shadow covers half the sky.
**Fire Breath** — once per battle: a full attack on two enemies standing one behind the other in a line.

**🐺 Werewolf Lord** — chief, melee · HP 8 · ATK 5 · SPD 7
Leader of the wolf packs of the Dead Thicket. He smells blood from three valleys away and never lets a wounded prey go.
**Call of Blood** — always, not once per battle: +1 ATK on every attack against an enemy that is already wounded.

**👹 Ogre Iron Fist** — chief, melee · HP 10 · ATK 4 · SPD 5
Once a blacksmith, now the fist of the Horde. His skull is harder than the anvil on which he forged swords.
**Hardhead** — once per battle, he ignores up to 2 damage from a single hit.

**🧙 Goblin Shaman** — henchman, shooter · HP 5 · ATK 1 · SPD 3
Gathers mushrooms, bones and bad dreams, then stirs them in a cauldron. His curses make even the bravest hands shake.
**Sinister Curse** — once per battle: the enemy hit has −1 ATK on its next attack. *(Curse token)*

**🕷️ Cave Spider** — henchman, melee · HP 6 · ATK 2 · SPD 3
Lives in the Dark Cave and weaves webs so strong that even birds get stuck in them.
**Spider Webs** — once per battle: an enemy within 4 hexes cannot move in its next activation. *(Immobilisation token)*

**🦇 Shadow Bat** — henchman, flying · HP 5 · ATK 2 · SPD 3
The quietest servant of the Horde. It drops out of the darkness before anyone hears the rustle of wings.
**Night Ambush** — once per battle: its attack deals +3 damage.

### Crystal guardians

> *A blue crest with a crystal. Neither good nor evil — they simply do not give back what has come to them.*

**👁️ Trinket Cyclops** — shooter · HP 23 · ATK 2 · SPD 2
A giant from a cave full of trinkets. He considers the Crystal of Care the most beautiful trinket in the world and throws boulders at anyone who reaches for it.
**Crushing Boulder** — after each hit it pushes the target 1 hex further away (if that hex is free).

**🦅 Treasure-Thief Griffin** — flying · HP 18 · ATK 3 · SPD 6
Steals everything that glitters, and snatched the crystal right out of the air. It attacks from the sky and immediately returns to its nest.
**Plundering Raid** — after each attack it may fly away up to 4 hexes.

**🐍 Hydra of the Sunken Ruins** — melee · HP 21 · ATK 2 · SPD 3
She slept in the flooded ruins until the crystal fell right under her nose. Now she guards it with all her heads at once.
**Maws of the Abyss** — with each attack it may hit two opponents standing next to it.

In the **duel on the board** the guardian fights with its second card, with a **sky blue back** (the ordinary guardian cards have a navy blue one): it has less Health on it and an ability for one-on-one fighting. **Cyclops** — HP 14, *Stone Skin*: every blow against the Cyclops deals 1 less damage. **Griffin** — HP 10, *Thief's Claw*: when it deals damage, it regains 1 HP. **Hydra** — HP 12, *Regrowing Heads*: at the start of each of its activations it regains 2 HP.

## 3. How to win

**The Dawn Covenant wins** when it carries all the needed crystals to the Crystal Tree (2 when the Covenant has one hero, 3 when it has two). Defeating the Horde's heroes is not enough — only crystals count.

**The Dark Horde wins** when:
- the last round runs out (limit: 8 / 12 / 18 / 24 rounds), **or**
- it defeats all the Covenant's heroes, **or**
- it performs the **ritual**: at the end of three consecutive rounds one of its heroes stands on the Tree, and all the other living ones stand on the hexes right next to the Tree.

> 🌳 *The Tree does not ask who is stronger. It only asks who gets there in time.*

What if the last heroes of both sides fall in the same battle? See [chapter 14](#14-special-cases).

## 4. Game level — before you start

Choose one of three levels. Nothing else needs to be set.

| Level | For whom | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 🌱 **Easy** | ages 5 and up, the first game | **Duels on the board** (chapter 9), **no abilities** and **no ritual**. A fallen hero is replaced by a **reserve** (chapter 13). |
| ⚔️ **Normal** | everyone | The full game: 3-on-3 battles in the arena, abilities and treasures. |
| 🔥 **Hard** | adults, co-op only | The full game, and the evil automaton has **one more hero** and starts with **2 random treasures**, but gets no reinforcements (chapter 12). |

At no level is a die rolled: a blow always deals as much damage as the Attack value. The die is an extra hardship for those who want it (chapter 13).

**The digital version** has complexity levels 0–3 (Walk, Adventure, Expedition, Settlement), and the difficulty (Easy, Normal, Hard) is chosen separately on levels 1–3. The "Easy" of the table above is, in the digital version, **level 0, Walk**: duels on the board, no abilities and no ritual, with its own points column. On levels 1–3 all difficulties play full arena battles and differ only in the computer's starting conditions (the number of treasures, the strength of the Horde): on Easy the computer starts weaker, on Hard stronger.

**Difficulty levels in the digital version** (chosen in every mode, including the arena): 🌱 **Easy** (the computer is handicapped: fewer treasures, a weaker Horde), ⚔️ **Normal** (the computer plays by the same rules as the players), 🔥 **Hard** (the computer has advantages: an extra hero and treasures, in co-op no reinforcements for the Horde, and at a table with bots also treasures at the start) and 🐉 **Very hard**. On Very hard the computer has an even bigger advantage than on Hard: **2 more random treasures** at the start, **half again as much income** and (on levels with settlements and heroes) extra **starting resources** (3 wood, 3 ore, 2 Dust) and **2 artifacts** for the main hero; its units have the same Health as yours. In the arena, Very hard is a computer that makes no mistakes in its choices. Whoever beats the computer on a higher level gets more ranking points (on Very hard 1.5 times as many as on Hard). The level icons: rabbit, wolf, bear and dragon.

On the easy level (in the board version: Easy, in the digital one: Walk) the rules fit on a single sheet: you walk around the map, flip tiles, and in a fight you take turns dealing blows.

## 5. Setup

1. **Choose a mode:**
   - **Co-op** (1–2 players): you all play the Covenant against the evil automaton (chapter 12).
   - **Competition** (2–6 players): some play the Covenant, the others the Horde; at most 3 players per faction.
2. **Choose the map length:**

   | Map | Rows of hexes | Treasures on the map | Covenant's starting treasures | Round limit |
   |---|---:|---|---|---:|
   | Short | 6 (21 hexes) | 1 Elixir of Rebirth + 1 other | — | 8 |
   | Medium | 8 (36 hexes) | 1 Elixir of Rebirth + 3 others | — | 12 |
   | Long | 11 (66 hexes) | 2 Elixirs of Rebirth + 4 others | 1 random (not an elixir) | 18 |
   | Very long | 14 (105 hexes) | 3 Elixirs of Rebirth + 5 others | 1 Elixir of Rebirth + 1 random | 24 |

   There are as many crystals on the map as need to be carried: 2 when the Covenant has one hero, 3 when it has two.

3. **Lay out the map** in the shape of a triangle:
   - The Tree at the top, the Covenant's base in the bottom left corner, the Horde's in the bottom right.
   - Fill the left half with face-down tiles from the green deck, the right half from the burgundy one. The hexes right in the middle belong alternately to one side and the other (in a zigzag).
   - Mix treasure tiles and **guardian lairs** in among them — as many lairs as there are crystals on the map. The Griffin lives on the Covenant's side, the Cyclops and the Hydra on the Horde's side. Put a crystal on each lair.
   - There are as many treasure tiles as there are treasures on the map (table above). **The map treasure pile:** set aside as many Elixirs of Rebirth and random other treasures as the table says, shuffle them and place them face down next to the map. Whoever steps onto a treasure tile takes the top card. The remaining treasure cards form the **treasure deck** — the Covenant draws its starting treasures from it, and the treasures left by guardians.
4. **Reveal** the Tree, both bases and the hexes adjacent to the bases.
5. **Each player chooses a hero** and puts it in their faction's base. The supports of both factions wait beside the board. On the long and very long map the Covenant draws its starting treasures (table above).
6. Put the **crystal marker** in slot "1" of the round track. Note which slot is the limit of your map (8, 12, 18 or 24 — those slots are highlighted).

## 6. How a round works

The game is played in **rounds**. In each round every living hero has one **turn**:

- **in competition** — in turns: a Covenant hero, a Horde hero, a Covenant hero…
- **in co-op** — first all the players, then the automaton's heroes.

When everyone has finished, it is the **end of the round**:

1. Is one Horde hero standing on the Tree, with the other living ones right beside it? If so — the ritual continues (chapter 7).
2. Was that the last round of your map? If so — the Horde wins.
3. Move the crystal marker one slot further.

> ⏳ *Each slot of the track is one day on which the glow of the Tree fades. Do not waste them.*

## 7. Travelling the land

### Movement

On its turn a hero has **3 movement points**. Unused points are lost.

| Terrain | Cost | Tiles |
|---|---|---|
| easy | 1 | Glade, Forest, Bridge · Scorched Earth, Barren Plain · treasures, lairs, bases, the Tree |
| hard | 2 | River, Hills · Dark Swamp, Dark Cave, Grim Ruins |
| obstacle | — | Mountains, Chasm · Dead Thicket, Poison Thorns |

> 🌿 *The Covenant's lands are gentler: on the bright side only 2 kinds of terrain are hard, on the dark side as many as 3.*

You can only enter **revealed** hexes. When a hero enters a hex, **flip all the face-down tiles around it**.

**Only one hero stands on a hex.** A hex occupied by a hero of your faction cannot be entered or passed through. Exception: **in your own base** several heroes may stand (that is how you start the game).

### What can happen

- **Treasure** — the hero picks it up. The treasure goes to the **faction's shared equipment** (chapter 11).
- **A crystal without a guardian** — a Covenant hero picks it up at once. It can carry any number of crystals. The Horde does not pick up crystals.
- **The Tree** — a Covenant hero who enters the Tree with crystals gives them all to it.
- **A guardian** — entering its hex is a challenge to a fight (chapter 10). Only a Covenant hero can do it.
- **An enemy hero** — entering its hex starts a **battle** (chapter 8). The winner stays on that hex.

Entering a hex with a fight costs as many movement points as the terrain cost of that hex — like every step. A battle does not end the move: if the hero wins and has points left, it can go on.

### Death of a hero

A fallen hero stays on the map as dead, and its player waits. **The crystals it carried stay on the battlefield** — the Horde can guard them but cannot take them.

A fallen hero can be resurrected by a **living** hero of its faction using an **Elixir of Rebirth**. The resurrected hero stands on a **chosen free hex next to** the one who used the elixir and returns to the game from the next round. If there is no free hex next to it, you must first move elsewhere. If all the heroes of a faction fall, the faction loses — an elixir in the equipment will no longer help.

### The ritual

At the end of every round check: is **one Horde hero standing on the Tree**, and are **all the other living Horde heroes standing on the hexes right next to the Tree**? (When the Horde has one hero, it is enough that it stands on the Tree.) If so — the ritual begins. If this holds at the end of **three consecutive** rounds, the Horde wins. If even one of them leaves or dies, the ritual is interrupted and you have to start over.

> 🌑 *They say that during the ritual the crystal leaves of the Tree darken one by one, like candles going out.*

An attack on a base full of heroes, a crystal underfoot, the ritual and resurrected heroes — the details are in [chapter 14](#14-special-cases).

### Example: a turn on the map

The Unicorn King stands on a Glade and has **3 movement points**.

1. He enters a **Forest** (cost 1, 2 points left). The players flip the face-down tiles around the Forest: **Hills** and a **treasure tile** appear.
2. He enters the **treasure** (cost 1, 1 point left) and takes the top card from the map treasure pile — it goes to the Covenant's shared equipment. **Mountains** and a **River** are revealed around it.
3. Hills and River cost 2 points each, and Mountains cannot be entered. The Unicorn ends his turn — the last point is lost.

**A fight in the middle of a move.** On the next turn, next to the Unicorn, on Scorched Earth, stands the Werewolf Lord. Entering his hex costs 1 point (as much as Scorched Earth) and starts a battle. The Unicorn wins, stays on that hex and still has 2 points — he can go on.

## 8. Battle in the arena

*(normal and hard level)*

> ⚔️ *When two teams meet in the land, the ground parts and carries them to an old arena — a green one if the fight broke out on the Covenant's lands, or a fiery one if on the Horde's.*

### Who fights

Each hero takes **two living supports** from its faction's shared pool (if fewer remain, as many as there are). The fight is **3 on 3**, and against a guardian **3 on 1**.

### Deployment

Each side puts its units on any hexes of its **outermost column** (the highlighted arena hexes). **The attacker deploys first**, the defender sees its deployment. The guardian stands in the middle of its column.

### Order

In every battle round each unit has **one activation**, from the fastest to the slowest. **With equal Speed the defender acts first.**

### Activation

1. **Move** — by as many hexes as the Speed value (you may stay in place). You may not pass through a hex occupied by another unit (ally or enemy) or through an obstacle. Flying units fly over everything, but land on a free hex.
2. **Attack** (if you want):
   - **melee** — an enemy on an adjacent hex;
   - **shooter** — any enemy that can be seen. An obstacle on the line hides the target, units do not.

### Damage

A blow deals as much damage as the **Attack** value. Put damage drops on the card. **There are no counterattacks.** When the damage equals the Health, the unit falls and leaves the arena.

### Abilities

The descriptions are on the cards. After using a "once per battle" ability, mark it with a **used ability** token. Mark effects that last until the next activation or attack with the matching token (Slow, Curse, Immobilisation, Attack Boost, +3 Attack). Remove the token when the effect is over — for example +3 Attack after the Dragon's attack.

### End of the battle

The battle lasts **until one side has lost all its units** — even if its hero fell earlier.

- Survivors **regain full Health**.
- Fallen **supports die for good** (unless an Elixir of Rebirth resurrects them).
- A fallen **hero** stays dead on the map (chapter 7).

The exact workings of abilities, shooting at close range and very long battles are described in [chapter 14](#14-special-cases).

### Example: the start of a battle

The Werewolf Lord steps onto the Unicorn King's hex on the Covenant's lands, so the fight takes place in the green arena. The Werewolf takes the Goblin Shaman and the Cave Spider, the Unicorn — the Elf Druid and the Nymph. The Horde attacks, so it deploys first; the outermost columns are 12 hexes apart.

**Order in every round:** Unicorn (SPD 8) → Werewolf (SPD 7) → Elf Druid and Nymph (SPD 4) → Goblin Shaman and Spider (SPD 3).

**Round 1**

- **The Unicorn** advances only 3 hexes. The Werewolf can move at most 7, so he will not reach him this round.
- **The Werewolf** runs 7 hexes. He is one hex short of the Unicorn — he does not attack.
- **The Elf Druid** shoots the Werewolf: 1 damage and Tangled Roots — a Slow token (−2 SPD in the Werewolf's next activation) and a used ability token at the Elf.
- **The Nymph** moves closer to the Unicorn.
- **The Goblin Shaman** shoots the Unicorn: 1 damage and Sinister Curse — a Curse token (−1 ATK on the Unicorn's next attack).
- **The Spider** moves closer. The Unicorn is further than 4 hexes away, so Spider Webs cannot reach him yet.

**Round 2**

- **The Unicorn** runs around the Werewolf and stops next to him — he moved 4 hexes, so Charge of Light works. The blow: 5 ATK + 2 (Charge) − 1 (Curse) = **6 damage**. The Werewolf has 7 damage against 8 Health. The Curse token comes off.
- **The Werewolf** is slowed, but he stands next to his target. The blow: 5 ATK + 1 (Call of Blood, because the Unicorn is already wounded) = **6 damage**. The Unicorn has 7 damage against 8 Health. The Slow token comes off.
- **The Elf Druid** shoots the Werewolf: 1 damage — **the Werewolf falls** and leaves the arena.
- **The Nymph** uses Gift of Life: the Unicorn regains 3 HP and now has only 4 damage.
- **The battle goes on.** The Goblin and the Spider fight although their chief has fallen — the battle ends only when one side loses all its units.

## 9. Duel on the board

*(easy level or the "duels on the board" setting)*

> 🌱 *The simplest fight: two heroes face each other and nobody runs away.*

- **Only two units** fight: hero against hero or hero against guardian. No supports, no arena, **no movement**.
- The one with the higher Speed strikes first. On a tie — the one who is defending.
- You take turns dealing blows equal to the Attack, until someone falls.
- **The guardian fights with the card with the sky blue back** (chapter 2): less Health and an ability for one-on-one fighting — the Cyclops' Stone Skin, the Griffin's Thief's Claw, the Hydra's Regrowing Heads. On the easy level abilities do not work: only the Health from this card counts.

## 10. Crystal guardians

> 🔷 *The guardians serve no one. They guard the crystals because crystals are pretty — and because nobody has managed to take them away yet.*

- Only the **Covenant** fights guardians. The Horde cannot enter a guardian's hex or pass through it.
- A guardian has **one activation per round**, like every unit. Its ability works **with every attack**, without limit.
- A guardian is tough: the team usually wins, but often loses a support in the process. It is wise to go to it with a **full team**.
- **Win:** the victorious hero stays on the lair and **picks up the crystal at once**. In addition **the guardian leaves a treasure**: the Covenant draws one random battle treasure from the deck that is not yet in play (not an Elixir of Rebirth and not a Magic Map).
- **Loss:** the hero dies on the guardian's hex, and the guardian stays there with full Health.

> 🦅 *The Griffin is called the Treasure Thief for a reason. In its nest there is always something glittering besides the crystal.*

**Who controls the guardian?** The guardian fights by the same rules as the evil automaton (chapter 12, "The automaton in battle"). In competition it is controlled by the **Horde player** (or any player not taking part in this fight). In co-op and in a solo game — by the same player who is fighting, honestly by the rules.

- **On the map** a treasure is used by a hero on its turn.
- **In battle** a treasure is used at the start of your own unit's activation, before it moves or attacks.

## 12. Playing against the evil automaton (co-op)

> 🌑 *The Horde never sleeps. Even when nobody commands it, its heroes go straight ahead — to the nearest victim or to the Tree.*

In co-op all the players lead the Covenant, and the Horde is led by simple rules. **The automaton has as many heroes as there are players** (on the hard level — one more, but at least three, and in addition 2 random treasures at the start). Draw them before the game.

**Horde reinforcements:** when the last living hero of the automaton falls, another one immediately appears in the Horde's base — an unused Horde hero, and when there are none left, one of the fallen (draw it). It enters the game from the next round. If the base is occupied by a Covenant hero, the new one appears on the nearest free hex. On the hard level there are no reinforcements.

### A Horde hero's turn

Check in order:

1. Does the Horde have an Elixir of Rebirth, and is one of its heroes dead? → drink it and resurrect the fallen one.
2. Is the hero standing on the Tree, or next to the Tree on which another Horde hero stands? → it stays there (guards the ritual).
3. Is a Covenant hero standing next to it? → it **attacks him**.
4. Otherwise it goes towards the **nearest** target: a Covenant hero or the Tree (when the Tree is occupied by another Horde hero — a free hex next to the Tree). On a tie it chooses the hero. It avoids guardians' hexes and hexes occupied by its own heroes.

### The automaton in battle

The automaton picks supports for the battle **at random** from the living ones. It puts the hero in the middle of the column, the supports above and below it.

Lead the automaton's units by simple rules:

- It uses **abilities** at the first opportunity.
- **Every unit** attacks the enemy with the **least Health** — among those it can reach without moving, and if there are none — among those it can reach in this activation. On a tie it chooses the hero.
- The automaton uses **treasures** at the first opportunity when they give something: the Sword, the Elixir of Vitality and the Stone of Protection go to the hero in its first activation; the Shield — to a hero with 5 Health or less left; the Elixir of Healing — to a unit that has lost half its Health; the Seed — when at least two of its units have lost 2 Health or more each; the Scroll of Magic Missile and Lightning — when they can finish off an enemy (Lightning also against two enemies); the Elixir of Rebirth — when its hero has fallen. The automaton does not use the other treasures.
- If a unit **can reach no one**, it goes towards the nearest enemy.

A **guardian** fights in the same way as the automaton's hero (chapter 10).

> 💡 The automaton does not avoid bad fights, but a wiped-out Horde returns from its base (except on the hard level, where it has one more hero from the start). If you want a bigger challenge, choose the **hard** level.

## 13. Custom settings

Once you know the game, you can pick individual settings instead of a ready-made level. Easings and hardships can be combined freely.

- **Battles:** in the arena (3 on 3) or duels on the board. Duels **favour the Covenant** — most on the short and medium map, and in competition more than in co-op.

**Easings:**

- **No abilities** — no unit, a guardian included, uses abilities.
- **Only Elixirs of Rebirth** — instead of the treasure deck, only Elixirs of Rebirth lie on the map (1 / 2 / 3 / 4 on the short / medium / long / very long map). Guardians leave nothing.
- **Reserve hero after death** — a player whose hero has fallen takes another, unused hero of their faction. The new hero enters on the **nearest free hex** to where the predecessor fell — one with no other hero and no guarded crystal. Entering costs no movement. In co-op the automaton also brings in a reserve.

**Hardships:**

- **Dice roll** — roll a die for every blow and add the result (−1, 0 or +1). A blow never deals less than 0 damage: with Attack 1 and a result of −1 it misses.
- **Extra Horde hero** (co-op) — the automaton has one more hero.

> ⚠️ In one-on-one competition on the long and very long map the **reserve hero favours the Horde**: a lone Horde hero often manages to perform the ritual before the Covenant pulls itself together.

## Tips for the first expeditions

> 🧭 *The Chroniclers of the Tree collected the advice of those who came back from expeditions — and of those who did not make it in time.*

### Dawn Covenant

- **Time is your enemy.** Keep an eye on the round track and count how many turns the way back to the Tree will take.
- **You win only with crystals.** A battle with a Horde hero costs supports and time — fight when it blocks the way or performs the ritual.
- **Go to a guardian with a full team.** A guardian often takes one support; without them the next battles will be hard.
- **Two heroes are two roads.** Split up: one explores the Covenant's side, the other the Horde's side.
- **Save the Elixir of Rebirth for a hero** — a fallen hero means lost turns.

### Dark Horde

- **The Tree is the best fortress.** As long as a Horde hero stands on the Tree, the Covenant will not give its crystals there.
- **Hunt the carrier.** The crystals of a fallen hero stay on the hex and have to be fetched — and that costs the Covenant time.
- **The ritual is frightening even when it fails.** The Covenant must turn back to interrupt it.

### In the arena

- **Hide shooters behind obstacles** and away from enemies: an enemy right next to a shooter forces the shooter to attack only him.
- **Leave the Unicorn King room for a run-up** — Charge of Light works after a move of at least 4 hexes.
- **Let the Ancient Bear stand next to the weakest ally** — Grove Guardian will take a deadly blow for it.
- **Hit those who are about to fall first.** Every unit fewer is one enemy activation fewer in every round.

## 14. Special cases

> 📜 *The Chroniclers of the Tree wrote down here everything players have argued about by the campfire. Look here when something is not obvious — in an ordinary game these rules are rarely needed.*

### On the map

- **Attack on a base where several heroes stand.** Only in a base can several heroes of one faction stand. The one with the most Health goes into the fight. If the attacker wins and an enemy still stands in the base, he **returns to the hex he came from**.
- **Battle for the Tree.** If a Horde hero stands on the Tree, a Covenant hero must defeat him first. He gives up the crystals only when there is no enemy left on the Tree.
- **A fallen hero's crystal.** The crystals of a fallen carrier lie on the battlefield. A Covenant hero who steps there **picks them up at once** — a free crystal never lies under a Covenant hero.
- **The last heroes of both sides fall in one battle.** The Covenant wins only with crystals, so when the last Covenant hero falls, the Horde wins — even if the last Horde hero fell in the same battle. (With a reserve hero, the reserve enters the game if there is still one.)
- **The ritual, resurrected heroes and the reserve.** All **living** Horde heroes count for the ritual. When one is resurrected or a reserve enters and is not standing on the Tree or right next to it — the ritual is interrupted.
- **A resurrected hero** stands on a chosen free hex next to the hero who used the elixir, and has a turn only in the next round.

### Reserve hero (the setting from chapter 13)

- If several free hexes are equally close to the place of death, the player **chooses one at the start of their turn**. If they forget and end the turn, the reserve appears on any of them.
- If the hero fell in a battle on their own turn, that turn is already used up. The reserve enters the board at the start of that player's **next** turn.

### In battle

- **A shooter with an enemy next to it.** If an enemy stands right next to a shooter, the shooter may attack only him.
- **Free abilities.** Sunlight, Gift of Life and Spider Webs are used during the activation, in addition to the move and the attack.
- **Charge of Light** works on every attack if in the same activation the Unicorn moved at least 4 hexes.
- **Fire Breath** — the Black Dragon attacks the enemy next to him and the enemy standing **right behind it** in the same line. Both take full Attack.
- **Call of Blood** works by itself on every attack of the Werewolf against an enemy that already has some damage.
- **Spider Webs** — an immobilised enemy cannot move in its next activation, but can attack.
- **Hardhead** works by itself: on the first hit for at least 2 damage the Ogre ignores 2 of it.
- **Grove Guardian** works by itself when the Bear or an adjacent ally is about to take a blow that would kill it. The Bear takes such a blow on himself (only if he survives it) and ignores 3 damage. Once per battle.
- **Order of shields**, when several work at once: first Grove Guardian, then Guardian's Shield (stops the whole blow), then Hardhead, last Stone of Protection (−1).
- **Guardians:**
  - **The Cyclops** shoots. After each hit it pushes the target 1 hex further away from itself, if that hex is free and is not an obstacle.
  - **The Griffin** may fly after each attack to a free hex up to 4 away.
  - **The Hydra** with each attack may hit two enemies standing next to it.
  - In a duel nobody moves, so Pegasus does not fly away, and the guardians fight with the cards with the sky blue back (their abilities from chapter 2 instead of the above).
- **Resurrection in battle.** An Elixir of Rebirth used in battle puts the unit with full Health on a free hex of your starting column.
- **A very long battle.** If a battle lasts 40 rounds, it ends: the side with the smaller sum of its units' Health in the arena loses (on an equal sum — the defender).

## 15. Arena — quick battle

> ⚔️ *You do not always have to cross the whole land. Sometimes a single battle is enough to see who is stronger.*

The Arena is a separate, short way to play: **one 3-on-3 battle, no map**, the Dawn Covenant against the Dark Horde. It takes about 5–10 minutes. In the computer version you can also play against the computer (Easy, Normal or Hard level).

**Setup**

1. One player takes the Dawn Covenant, the other the Dark Horde. Each chooses a **hero** of their faction and **2 of the 3 supports**. The lineup is open. The third support stays in reserve — it will come in handy for the Guardians' Horn.
2. Prepare the **treasure deck**: one of each treasure, without the Magic Map, and **2 Elixirs of Rebirth** — 15 cards in all. Shuffle it.
3. Flip a coin: the winner is **player A**. Also flip for which side of the arena board you fight on (the Covenant's or the Horde's).

**Drawing treasures**

The players choose in turn in the order **A, B, B, A**. The chooser reveals cards from the top of the deck until they have **3 different treasures** in front of them — the Horde player **4** — (a second identical Elixir is set aside), **takes one**, and shuffles the rest back into the deck. The Horde sees more cards because in the arena the Covenant is slightly stronger. Everyone ends up with **2 treasures** and keeps them hidden until they use them.

**Battle**

You fight as in chapter 8. Player A deploys their units first, in the left outermost column, then player B in the right one. **Initiative tie:** in odd battle rounds the unit of player A acts first, in even ones — of player B. Treasures work as in an ordinary battle.

**The end**

The side whose unit is the last one standing in the arena wins. If the last units of both sides fall at the same moment, it is a **draw**.

**The full 6-on-6 arena (computer version only, game without a network)**

In the new game wizard, in Arena mode, you can choose "6 on 6 (full)" instead of "3 on 3 (quick)". The setup changes:

1. Each side gets a **fixed pool of eight supports** of its faction (units of tiers 2–5, both lines) and chooses **five** of them. With the hero that makes six units per side. The sides deploy in two starting columns.
2. Before you choose supports, each hero takes **one artifact, chosen from three** (always from different body slots and with an effect in battle), and then **chooses two spells out of six** from circles I–II (Magic Missile, Healing, Slow, Stone Skin, Fireball, Surge). Mana is the same as in the full game.
3. Treasures from the deck are drawn as in the ordinary arena. The rest of the battle is unchanged.
4. The computer's levels get a little support: **Easy** one extra artifact, **Hard** +1 mana, **Very hard** plays without mistakes, and its hero additionally has +2 mana, one more spell and two extra artifacts. You have as many units as it does.

In the quick 3-on-3 arena on the **Very hard** level the computer draws one more treasure.

## 16. Cheat sheet

**1. Goal of the game**

**The Dawn Covenant wins when** it delivers **2 crystals** to the Tree (one Covenant hero) or **3** (two heroes). Defeating the Horde is not enough.

**The Dark Horde wins when** the round limit runs out (**8 / 12 / 18 / 24** for the short / medium / long / very long map), it defeats all the Covenant's heroes or performs the ritual.

**2. How a round works**

Every living hero has **1 turn per round**.

**Competition:** in turns — Covenant, Horde, Covenant… **Co-op:** first the players, then the automaton's heroes.

**At the end of the round:** 1) check the ritual; 2) check the round limit; 3) move the round marker by 1 slot.

**The ritual:** one Horde hero stands on the Tree, and all the other living Horde heroes on the adjacent hexes. If the condition holds at the end of three consecutive rounds, the Horde wins. If even one leaves or dies — the ritual starts over.

**3. Movement on the map**

On a turn a hero has **3 movement points**; unused ones are lost.

**Cost 1:** Glade, Forest, Bridge, Scorched Earth, Barren Plain, treasure, lair, base, the Tree.

**Cost 2:** River, Hills, Dark Swamp, Dark Cave, Grim Ruins. **Cannot be entered:** Mountains, Chasm, Dead Thicket, Poison Thorns.

You can only enter **revealed hexes**. After entering, reveal all the face-down tiles around. One hero stands on a hex; the exception is your own base.

**Treasure:** into the shared equipment. **A free crystal:** the Covenant picks it up. **The Tree:** the Covenant gives up all the crystals it carries, if there is no enemy there. **A guardian:** a fight only for the Covenant. **An enemy:** a battle. Entering a hex with a fight costs as much as the terrain cost; after a win the hero can go on if it has points left.

**4. Battle in the arena**

**Teams:** a hero + 2 living supports of the faction (if that many remain). Against a guardian: 3 on 1.

**Deployment:** the attacker deploys first; each side chooses hexes in its outermost column. The guardian stands in the middle of its column.

**Order:** every unit activates once per round, from the highest **Speed**. On a tie the defender acts first.

**Activation:** a move up to a number of hexes equal to the Speed (you may stay in place), then an optional attack. You do not pass through other units (allies or enemies) or obstacles. Flying units fly over everything, but land on a free hex.

**Attack:** melee — an enemy on an adjacent hex; shooter — a visible enemy. Obstacles block the line of fire, units do not. If an enemy stands next to a shooter, the shooter may attack only him.

**Damage = Attack.** There are no counterattacks. Mark the damage on the card; when it equals the Health, the unit falls. In the "Dice roll" variant add the result −1, 0 or +1 (minimum 0 damage).

The battle lasts until one side loses all its units. After the battle survivors regain full Health; fallen supports die for good; a fallen hero stays dead on the map.

**5. Guardians and treasures**

**Guardians:** only the Covenant fights them. Their ability works with every attack. After a victory the hero picks up the crystal, and the Covenant draws 1 random battle treasure (not an Elixir of Rebirth or a Magic Map). After a defeat the hero dies and the guardian regains full Health.

**Treasures are shared by the faction** and work once, then return to the box. On the map a hero uses them on its turn; in battle — at the start of a unit's activation, before it moves and attacks.

**Elixir of Rebirth:** resurrects a fallen unit. On the map the hero returns on a free hex next to the one using the elixir and acts from the next round. In battle it returns with full Health on a free hex of your starting column.

**6. Remember**

• **The Horde does not pick up crystals.**
• A Covenant hero will not give the crystals to the Tree while a Horde hero stands on it.
• When the last Covenant hero falls, the Horde wins — even if the last Horde hero fell in the same battle.
• **Duel on the board:** only two units, no movement and no supports. The faster one attacks first; on a tie — the defender. The guardian fights with the card with the sky blue back.

## 17. Level 3: Settlement — gold, settlements and the party (digital version)

This chapter applies only to the digital version, the "Settlement" level. It adds to the rules of the "Expedition" level: gold, settlements, buildings and recruitment.

### Gold
There is one currency: gold. Each faction has a shared purse, and its players also share treasures and supports. **Income** arrives at the end of the round:
- every settlement you hold (the starting one too): **2 gold**,
- a treasury in a settlement: **+1 gold**,
- a gold mine: **1 gold**.

Gold is also given by chests (**you choose: an artifact or 3 gold**). Fights with guardians and encounters give treasures, but not gold. When a hero is defeated the gold is not lost.

### Settlements and mines
A settlement has the faction imposed by the half of the map, but it **belongs to whoever stood on it** (an empty settlement is taken without a fight, a guarded one first requires defeating the defenders). A settlement keeps its buildings. An owner of a faction other than the settlement's gets its income, market and hospital, but **does not recruit**. A mine is held by whoever stands on it.

### Buildings (you have to buy them)
- **Barracks** (3 gold, upgrade 6 gold): recruitment of supports; the upgraded ones give an extra place in the party and supports of tiers 4 and 5.
- **Market** (3 gold, great 5 gold): artifacts for gold (common 2 gold, rare 5 gold, legendary 10 gold), 3 on offer (5 after the upgrade), a new offer every 3 rounds.
- **Hospital** (3 gold, upgraded 5 gold): resurrects a fallen support for half its price (after the upgrade for a third).
- **Treasury** (4 gold): +1 gold per round.

You can buy without a limit per turn; only the price limits you. The buildings are shown in the settlement view (click a building to use it).

### The party and supports
At the start there are 2 places in the party, then +1 at hero level 3, +1 at level 6 and +1 from the upgraded barracks, 5 at most in all. Each faction has 7 supports: the three existing ones (tier 1) and one new one at each of tiers 2 to 5 (3, 5, 8 and 12 gold, working prices). In the army there is one support of each kind. Covenant: Porcupine (Spikes), Centaur (Shot and Gallop), Alabaster Boar (Frontal Charge), Tree Guardian (Tree Roots). Horde: Ratman (Poisoned Claw), Hellhound (Manhunt), Ash Salamander (Blaze), Minotaur Chief (Battle Frenzy). The ability texts are on the cards.

### Artifact sets
Four sets of three rare pieces each (Forester's Outfit, Dawn Panoply, Sentinel's Plate, Merchant's Outfit) drop only on this level. A complete 3 of 3 gives a bonus; the hero card shows "2/3 of the set". Pieces are put on by hand. Details: `ZESTAWY_ARTEFAKTOW.md`.

### The evil automaton in co-op
A Horde led by the automaton does not collect gold: every few rounds its fallen supports return.

### Autopilot for a child
In family mode (the "Child" field in the game settings, that is, a parent's consent) the child's hero shops in settlements on its own: following the bots' rules it buys buildings, supports and artifacts, and opens a chest by taking the artifact.

## 18. Level 4: Principality — resources and huts (digital version)

This chapter applies only to the digital version, the "Principality" level. It adds to the rules of the "Settlement" level: three resources, more expensive buildings, quests in huts, new places on the map and the defence of settlements.

### Resources
Besides gold there are **wood**, **ore** and **Star Dust**. All of them are shared by the faction, like gold. The map provides them: a **sawmill** (+1 wood per round), an **ore mine** (+1 ore) and a rare **Star Dust spring** (+1 Dust). Mines are taken over like gold mines: whoever enters holds them until someone else enters the same hex (without a fight, unless the owner stands on it). At the start a faction has 3 wood.

### What costs resources
Gold pays for units; wood and ore for buildings, and Star Dust only for rare things.
- **Barracks:** 3 gold + 2 wood; upgrade 6 gold + 2 wood + 2 ore + 1 Dust.
- **Market:** 3 gold + 2 wood; great 5 gold + 2 ore + 1 Dust.
- **Hospital:** 3 gold + 2 wood; upgraded 5 gold + 2 ore + 1 Dust.
- **Treasury:** 4 gold + 2 ore.
- **Forge** (a new building of level 4): 4 gold + 3 ore; Great Forge 6 gold + 3 ore + 2 Dust.
- **A legendary artifact at the market:** 10 gold + 1 Dust.

**In a round a faction may build or upgrade only one building** (the button shows when you have already done it). The stocks and the income of resources are visible in the bottom bar, and the price on the build button.

### The Forge
The **Forge** arms the supports of the whole faction: they fight with **+1 Attack**, and after the upgrade to the Great Forge also with **+1 Health**. The best forge the faction has counts (two forges do not give a double bonus). Whoever loses a settlement with a forge loses the bonus too.

### Exchanging resources
When one resource is missing, you can **buy it for gold** in every settlement of your own (the Market tab): wood and ore for 4 gold each, Star Dust for 10 gold. A built **market** lowers the prices (3 gold and 8 gold, a great market 2 gold and 6 gold) and lets you **sell** surpluses: wood and ore for 1 gold each, Dust for 3 gold.

### Huts with quests
On the map stand huts. A hero who stands in a hut can do its **quest**: the hut asks for gold, a resource or an artifact from the backpack, and gives more in return (about one and a half times as much, in a different currency): gold, a resource, experience or an artifact. Each hut gives its quest once per game.

### The mill, the depot and the well
On the map there are also three kinds of places that help whoever enters them:
- **The Mill** gives 2 gold, and then grinds for 3 rounds (the icon is dimmed in the meantime).
- **The Forest Depot** gives up its stock once: 3 wood, 3 ore or 1 Star Dust.
- **The Wanderers' Well** gives a hero 2 movement points this turn; it works once per round, for the first one who drinks from it.

### The settlement window and the building tree
A settlement opens **full screen**: the faction's landscape is the background, the buildings stand on it, below are the services of the chosen building and the garrison, and a hero who stands in the settlement has their portrait here. The **Settlement Council** (it stands in every settlement from the start and costs nothing) opens the **building tree**: the nodes are buildings, the arrows lead from the building that must stand to the one that depends on it. A node is built, buyable (green), too expensive (red) or blocked (grey, the caption says what is missing). Click a node to see the price and the effect, and build it with the button under the tree; Esc returns to the landscape.

Dependencies: **Barracks, Market and Treasury** come straight from the Council; **Forge, Moat and Walls, and Hospital** require Barracks; the **Mage Tower** requires the Hospital; the **Tavern** the Market. You can build **any number of buildings per round**: only the price limits you.

### Defending a settlement: garrison and fortifications
A hero standing in their own settlement can leave up to **three supports** in it as a **garrison** (the settlement window, the Moat tab) and take them back when they return. The enemy will not take a settlement with a garrison just by entering: they must fight a **battle for the settlement**. A garrison without a hero in battle is commanded by the **player who owns the settlement** (with several players on that side, the one whose hero moved most recently; a human has precedence over a bot); if your hero also stands in the settlement, the garrison fights together with him.

The **Moat** building (4 gold + 3 wood) and its upgrade, **Walls with Turrets** (6 gold + 4 ore + 1 Dust), work in the battle for a settlement:
- **A moat with spikes:** whoever enters it ends their turn and takes 2 damage (the same if they end their next turn in it). You cross dry-footed only by the bridge in the middle. Flying units fly over the moat.
- **A wall with a gate:** nobody passes through it (flying units fly over), but arrows fly over the wall.
- **Two turrets:** at the start of every round each shoots the weakest attacker (1 damage).
- **The catapult:** the attacker always has it. Once per round, on the turn of any of their units, they point at a highlighted wall, turret or gate: a wall and a turret fall at once, the gate after two hits.

### The "Conquest" goal
On the Principality level you can play for **Conquest**. The side wins that at the end of a round holds **all the settlements on the enemy's land**: its capital (the settlement it starts from) and the settlements to be taken on its half of the map. The counter in the goal panel shows how many you already have. When the round limit passes, the side holding more than half of the enemy's settlements wins; if nobody does or both do, the Horde wins. Remember defence: leave garrisons and build fortifications, because the opponent is coming for your settlements.

### Family mode: autopilot
When "Child" is ticked by a hero, the younger player **travels and fights**, and does not have to deal with settlements and resources. If an adult plays the same faction, they build, recruit and trade (gold and resources are shared). If there is no adult, the **autopilot** does it, as a computer would. The child's chests open by themselves.

## 19. Level 5: Kingdom — a second hero and magic (digital version)

The "Kingdom" level adds to the rules of the Principality a **commander**, that is, the player's second hero.

### The tavern and hiring
In a settlement you can build a **tavern** (5 gold + 3 wood). In the tavern each player hires **one commander** for 8 gold. There are three types to choose from: **warrior** (tough, fights in melee), **archer** (shoots from afar) and **sage** (hits weaker, but heals or weakens). The commander gets their own name.

### What the commander can do
The commander is a full hero: has their own pawn on the map, their own turn (right after your main hero, from the round after hiring), gains experience and levels, chooses skills and wears artifacts. In battle they take supports from the same shared party of the faction. Heroes standing next to each other can pass artifacts to each other.

### When the commander falls
The commander does not die for good. They lose their artifacts (the victorious hero takes them), but keep their level and skills. From the next round you can **buy them back in the tavern**: 3 gold and 2 gold for every level of theirs. Buying back works only when your main hero is alive. **The death of the main hero is final**, but if the commander is alive, you play on and can win.

### Magic
Every hero has **mana**: 3 and 1 more for every level (sages 3 more). At the end of the round 1 point of mana returns. Main heroes know **Magic Missile** from the start; further spells are taught by the **Mage Tower** (a building in a settlement: 5 gold + 2 ore + 1 Dust; each upgrade opens the next **circle** of spells: the Great Mage Tower (8 gold + 3 ore + 3 Dust) the second, the Archmage Tower (11 gold + 4 ore + 4 Dust) the third). A first-circle spell costs 3 gold, a second-circle one 5 gold and 1 Star Dust, a third-circle one 8 gold and 2 Dust. The hero whose turn it is learns.

**In battle** a hero can cast **one spell** in its activation, in addition to the move and the attack: choose a spell from the "Spells" bar, then point at a target.
- **Magic Missile** (2 mana): 3 damage to any enemy.
- **Healing** (2): an ally regains 4 Health.
- **Slow** (1): an enemy has −2 Speed in its next activation.
- **Stone Skin** (2, second circle): until the end of the battle blows against an ally are 1 weaker.
- **Fireball** (3, second circle): 2 damage to the target and everyone next to it, yours too.
- **Surge** (3, second circle): an ally immediately gets an extra activation.
- **Thunder** (4, third circle): 5 damage to a chosen enemy, and the lightning jumps to the nearest other enemy (2 damage).
- **Curse** (3, third circle): an enemy has −1 Attack until the end of the battle (not below 1).
- **Shield** (3, third circle): the first blow against a chosen ally is absorbed completely.
- **Inspiration** (3, third circle): an ally has +2 Attack in its next activation.

**On the map**, on the hero's turn (buttons under their name): **Vision** (2 mana) reveals hexes within a radius of 3, **Quicken** (2) gives the hero +2 movement points this turn, and **Portal** (4, second circle) moves the hero to the nearest settlement of their own and ends their move.

Spells grow with the hero: every 3 levels they deal and heal 1 more; the sage has +1 from the start.

### Hero type and speciality
On the Kingdom level every hero has a **type**, which strengthens them by itself at each promotion:
- **warrior** (Ancient Bear, Ogre Iron Fist, warrior commanders): +1 Health per level,
- **hunter** (Unicorn King, Werewolf Lord, archer commanders): +1 Attack every two levels,
- **sage** (Golden Dragon, Black Dragon, sage commanders): +1 mana per level and stronger spells.

Every hero also has a **speciality**, that is, one bonus of their own (for example the Unicorn King has +1 movement point on the map, and the Ogre gets 1 gold for every battle won). The type and speciality are visible on the hero card.

### Skill branches
Four skills (Power, Mettle, Lightness and Commander) can be completed in two ways. When you have such a skill at tier II and its tier III shows up on a promotion, an extra card lies beside it, the **branch**: instead of the last point for the hero you get something else (a bonus for supports, healing every round or a wider deployment). The choice is final.

## 20. Level 6: Empire — the Relic and factions (digital version)

The "Empire" level adds the **Shards of the Primal Spark** to the rules of the Kingdom.

### Shards
On the map, on hexes visible from the start, lie glowing **Shards of the Primal Spark** (from 2 on the smallest map to 6 on the largest). A hero who enters such a hex takes the Shard. A Shard is not put on like an artifact: the hero simply carries it, and the counter under their name shows how many they carry themselves, how many the whole faction has and how many are in the game.

**Whoever defeats a hero takes all their Shards.** If a hero falls in a fight with a guardian or a garrison, their Shards fall on the adjacent hex and anyone can pick them up.

### The Primal Spark building
When **one hero carries all the Shards** and stands in their own settlement, they can raise the Primal Spark building there: the **Sanctuary** (Covenant) or the **Black Forge** (Horde). The price is all the Shards. Shards scattered among several heroes are not enough: a hero can **pass their Shards** to an ally standing on the same or an adjacent hex (a button in the hero panel on the map). The Sanctuary gives all the Covenant's units +2 Health in every battle, the Black Forge all the Horde's units +1 Attack.

### Faction mechanics and faction buildings
On the Empire level each faction fights in its own way, but **to have its mechanic you have to build the building that gives it** (the best tier in the whole faction):
- **Shrine of Dawn (Covenant, requires the Hospital), 4 tiers:** I Light of Dawn (at the start of every battle round the most badly wounded unit regains 1 Health); II heals 2; III heals two units; IV all the Covenant's units start the battle with +1 Health.
- **Blood Pit (Horde, requires the Forge), 4 tiers:** I Bloodlust (when a Horde enemy dies in battle, all its units get +1 Attack until the end of the battle, once per battle); II twice; III three times; IV with Bloodlust the Horde's units also regain 1 Health.

Each faction also has two more buildings: the **Covenant** the Tree Nursery (+1 wood per round per tier, adds up with settlements; requires the Treasury) and the Grove Circle (a hero entering the hex of their own settlement restores all their mana; requires the Mage Tower); the **Horde** the Plunder House (+2 gold after winning against a hero or guardian, adds up; requires the Market) and the Wolf Den (the Horde's heroes have +1 movement point; requires the Moat). All of them are visible in the building tree under the Settlement Council.

### Two units per tier and dwellings
On the Empire level each of the unit tiers 2–5 has **two units to choose from**. The Barracks on the Empire level stand in every settlement from the start. Before you recruit a unit of such a tier, build its **dwelling** (it is a building; in the tree under the Settlement Council they hang one after another under the Barracks: the tier 3 dwelling requires the tier 2 dwelling and so on). **The dwelling of one unit rules out the other unit of that tier until the end of the game**, so choose the one that suits your party.
- Covenant: Porcupine or Silver Fox; Centaur or Dawn Owl; Alabaster Boar or Dryad Archer; Tree Guardian or White Stag.
- Horde: Ratman or Goblin Slinger; Hellhound or Gargoyle; Ash Salamander or Cave Troll; Minotaur Chief or Shadow Wyvern.

### The legendary unit
In the upgraded barracks, for 20 gold and 3 Star Dust, a faction can recruit one legendary unit: the **Phoenix of Dawn** (flies; once per battle after dying it rises with half its Health) or the **Behemoth of Doom** (with each attack it may hit two adjacent enemies).

### The "Relic" goal
In a game for the Relic **raising the Primal Spark building wins at once**. When the round limit passes, the side that carries more Shards wins (on an equal number the Horde).
